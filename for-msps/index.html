For MSPs

Why your PSA and your general ledger drift apart.

An MSP runs on two systems that were never designed to agree. Contracts, hours and billing live in the PSA. Revenue, costs and cash live in the accounting system. A sync connects them, and the sync is where the money goes missing.

Get your PSA-to-GL Drift Report

Where it breaks

Six places we look first.

These are common patterns, not a list of anyone's mistakes. Most MSPs have at least three of them.

  • 01Invoices that never fully arriveAgreement invoices are generated in the PSA, but some don't sync, sync twice, or post to the wrong income account. The PSA total and the GL total stop matching, quietly.
  • 02A mapping set up once and never touched againNew products and service types default to a catch-all account. Over time, revenue by service line in the P&L stops meaning anything.
  • 03Credits and voids handled on one side onlyA credit memo or a voided invoice fixed in the accounting system but not in the PSA, or the other way around. Both systems are now right about different things.
  • 04Prepaid blocks recognized too earlyPrepaid hours and retainers booked as revenue when invoiced, instead of deferred and recognized as the hours are used. MRR looks better than it is.
  • 05Resale costs never matched to billingDistributor bills for licenses and hardware land as one big expense. Nobody compares seats paid for with seats billed, so unbilled licenses go unnoticed.
  • 06Labor that never reaches the agreementTechnician time lives in the PSA; payroll lives in accounting. Without allocating labor to agreements, gross margin per client can't be calculated.

What it costs you

An MRR number you can't defend, margin per client you can't see, a close that takes weeks, and due diligence findings that arrive at the worst possible time: when you're selling, buying or borrowing.

What you can check yourself this week

Pick last month. Total the invoices your PSA says it generated, and total the income your accounting system shows for the same period, by account. If you can't explain the difference in a few minutes, the gap is real. That's what the Drift Report quantifies, invoice by invoice.
